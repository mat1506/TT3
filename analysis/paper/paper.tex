  \begin{abstract}
  Paleohydrological reconstructions from the Chilean Altiplano document
  abrupt moisture fluctuations during the last millennia. Although the
  end of the mid Holocene aridity and the onset of more humid conditions
  between 6--4 ka has been identified in numerous regional marine and
  terrestrial sites, the timing of late Holocene dry and humid phases
  shows large regional variability. Laguna Miscanti and Laguna Miñiques
  (23\(^\circ\) 43'S -- 67\(^\circ\) 46'W, 4140 m asl) are
  topographically closed, but connected by surface outflow from
  Miscanti. Sedimentological and geochemical indicators from two new
  cores show large facies changes, i.e.~higher carbonate and evaporite
  deposition during more arid periods and increased organic productivity
  (both algal and macrophyte) during more humid phases. As in most
  Andean lakes located in volcanic settings, large 14C reservoir effects
  occur complicating 14C dating, so the age models include 210Pb and
  U/Th dating. In spite of dating uncertainties, both lakes show similar
  patterns during the last millennium. A humid phase in Laguna Miscanti
  prior to ca 1200 CE is coherent with rodent middens and
  geomorphological features indicative of a major pluvial/recharge event
  at lower altitudes (Atacama Desert) during the Medieval Climate
  Anomaly (ca 800 - 1300 CE). The LIA (1300 -- 1850 CE) is characterized
  by several arid/humid cycles and the last century by a productivity
  increase. The hydrological changes observed during the last millennium
  illustrate the complex dynamics of recent climate evolution over the
  high altitude Andean plateau. Discrepancies between the northern and
  southern Altiplano records and with intermediate latitudes (Central
  Chile) records may reflect contrasting responses to external forcing
  (Westerlies versus South American Monsoon dynamics) along different
  climatic zones.
  \end{abstract}

\hypertarget{sedimentological-facies-and-elemental-geochemistry}{%
\subsection{Sedimentological facies and elemental
geochemistry}\label{sedimentological-facies-and-elemental-geochemistry}}

In November 2019, we recovered a sedimentary sequence
(\textasciitilde2.8 m deep) in an area of 15 x 10 cm following the
archaeological strata of the profile exposed on the western wall of a
1x2 m excavation in the ALTT (Tagua-Tagua 3, Figure 1). Sedimentary
facies were defined and characterized based on macroscopic description
(color, bedding features, grain size, lithology and sedimentary
textures). Sediment samples ---between 5 to 10 g of material--- were
obtained at 1-cm intervals. Samples were sent to the laboratories of
Pyrenees Ecology Institute (IPE-CSIC) and the Andalusian Institute Earth
Sciences Institute (IACT-CSIC) in Spain for geochemical, pollen and
isotopic analysis. Other samples group were sent to the Archaeobotany
and Palaeoecology Laboratory of the University of Exeter for the
processing and extraction of diatom and phytolith.

Subsamples were taken every 2 cm for analysed of total carbon (TC),
total inorganic carbon (TIC), total organic carbon (\(TOC = T-TIC\)),
total nitrogen (TN) and total sulfur (TS) in a LECO SCN furnace
(IPE--CSIC). The grain-size frequency distributions (between 0.02 um and
2000 um) were measured in triplicate using a Malvern Mastersizer 2000
APA2000 grain-size analyzer with Hydro 2000G AWA2000. To determine the
grain--size, the samples were pre-treated with 10\% H2O2 and 10\% HCl to
remove organic matter and carbonates, respectively. Robust End-member
mixing analysis (EMMA) was performed using the generic deterministic
EMMA protocol slightly modified (R package EMMAgeo, version: 0.9.8.
Dietze et al 2012; Dietze and Dietze 2019) to infer the sediment
sources, transport and depositional processes.

\hypertarget{diatoms-analysis}{%
\subsection{Diatoms analysis}\label{diatoms-analysis}}

Approximately 1 g of dry sediment every 2 cm were processed followingthe
Queen's University methods. Clastic sediments were removed using a
sodium polytungstate treatment. Permanent slides were prepared with
Naphrax (Battarbee et al., 2002). Diatoms were identified and quantified
under a trinocula Carl Zeiss microscope, AxioLab A1, with an oil
immersion objective 1000x. Quantitative analyses were done by
calculating relative abundances by counting approximately 200 valves (up
to 900 valves were counted in some levels down to 30 when diatoms were
scarce) in continuous transects encompassing the whole slide. Diatoms
were classified to species or variety level. Optical microscopy images
(1000x) were taken using a digital SLR camera (Canon EOS Rebel) attached
to microscope. The nomenclature status of species or variety was
verified using the Catalogue of Diatom Names (California Academy of
Sciences). Diatoms were grouped according to life forms and ecological
characteristics. Data were analyzed and plotted using `tidypaleo'
version 0.1.1 packets in R 4.1.0 software.

\hypertarget{pollen-analysis-and-macrocarbon-analysis}{%
\subsection{Pollen Analysis and Macrocarbon
analysis}\label{pollen-analysis-and-macrocarbon-analysis}}

Eleven pollen samples were obtained from short core VIC11-2A-1G and
processed following Faegri and Iversen (1989) methods to extract pollen
grains. Lycopodium tablets were added for calculating pollen
concentration (grains/cm3; Stockmarr(1971)) and accumulation rates
(grains/cm2/yr). Pollen grains were mounted on glass slides and
identified and quantified under an Axiostar Carl Zeiss microscope (400x
and 1000x) using published pollen atlas Heusser (1971) and the reference
pollen collection of the Paleoecology Laboratory at CEAZA. Pollen counts
include 300 terrestrial pollen grains excluding paludal taxa. Data were
analyzed and plotted using Tilia software.

\hypertarget{phytoliths}{%
\subsection{Phytoliths}\label{phytoliths}}

Phytoliths were obtained from samples of approximately 2 gr of sediment,
spaced every 30 cm on the 8 long sediment cores. We followed the
standard procedures for phytoliths extraction (Piperno, 1988, 1994,
2006) using 36\% HCl and 70\% HNO3 to remove carbonates and organic
matter respectively. Phytoliths were separated by heavy liquid flotation
using ZnBr2 prepared to a density of 2.3 g/cm3 (Dickau et al., 2013).
Finally, phytoliths were counted until obtaining a total of 200 per
sample and they were classified according to the International Code for
Phytolith Nomenclature (Madella et al.~2005). The presence of maize leaf
phytoliths was studied using the discriminant function of cross-shaped
phytoliths (cross variants 1 and 5/6) (Piperno et al., 2006). The
percentage of each family or subfamily was plotted and the zones were
built through the CONISS method carried out with the Rioja package in
the R software (Juggins, 2012). Additionally, a change point analysis
was carried out in the sequence of arboreal phytoliths to identify
possible significant increases or decreases of this vegetation cover in
the studied basin (Bai and Perron, 2003; Killick and Eckley, 2014).
